## The idea

- What it is: a two-sided HoReCa ecosystem — a B2B platform connecting restaurants with suppliers, staff and premises, plus a free B2C diner app for discovery, booking, QR payment and verified reviews that feeds restaurants customer data, loyalty and marketing tools.
- B2B scope: a listings marketplace only (suppliers, jobs, properties); it connects the parties and does not run the deals. Decided: B2B listings are a free hook that brings restaurants onto the platform; revenue comes from the QR payment, guest data and loyalty product.
- Who it is for: first, independent restaurants in Georgia (B2B side: restaurant owners and their suppliers of goods and services); diners in Georgia (B2C side).
- What it sells, at what price: tiered subscriptions for restaurant owners and suppliers starting from 100 GEL/month, with a free tier with limited functions. During the MVP stage everything is free. A fee on QR payments is under evaluation and may be bundled into subscription tiers. The diner app is free.
- Where and how: Georgia (starting city not yet specified). Delivered as web/mobile apps. Customer acquisition channels not yet specified.
- Budget and constraints: not yet specified (budget, team, technical skills, timeline and existing industry relationships are open).

### Decisions made since (Nov 2026)
- Starting city: **Tbilisi**, table-service independent restaurants first (upscale and tourist areas: Sololaki, Old Tbilisi, Vera, Vake).
- Paying wedge: QR pay at the table → private verified dish ratings → guest profiles and loyalty (`offer.md`).
- Price: **Guest Loop 39 GEL/month**, Pro 99 GEL; Founding Restaurants 29 GEL locked for 12 months after 3 free months (`pricing.md`). The original idea was 100 GEL+.
- Payment through a licensed partner (Keepz / Bank of Georgia / TBC); the platform never holds guest money (`ops.md`).
- Founder builds alone in Lovable; working brand name **Dabrundi** (`brand.md`); launch 1 Feb 2027 (`launch.md`).

### Risks every member raised

1. **Too many products at once.** All three counted 7 to 10 separate products (sourcing, HR, property, directory, booking, payments, reviews, profiles, loyalty, campaigns), serving at least four kinds of buyer. Each product has its own incumbents, so every one is a fight, every one comes out mediocre, and there is no single result for anyone to pay for.
2. **Cold start on every side.** The valuable part (diner profiles, repeat-visit data, loyalty) needs many diners, and diners need many restaurants. Suppliers need restaurants. A free MVP postpones this problem but doesn't solve it. There's no plan yet for how the first 100 restaurants or the first diners arrive.
3. **QR payment carries all the risk.** Verified reviews, profiles and loyalty only exist if diners actually pay through the product. Two risks sit on this one step: an app-install prompt at the end of the meal (Product), and the work of processing payments and getting licensed for it (Monopoly, Offers).
4. **Willingness to pay, and market size, are unknown.** Nobody knows yet whether independent Georgian restaurants feel this pain enough to pay 100 GEL or more a month, or how many there are in the starting city (still not named).

### Where they disagree

- **Product votes FUND IF; Offers and Monopoly vote PASS.** Product sees one clean, buildable loop and would back a pilot. The other two say that even the narrowed version still has to prove distribution, willingness to pay and defensibility before anyone funds it.
- **The moat.** Monopoly sees payment-linked, dish-level data as the one real moat, but one that only forms after scale. Offers treats it as proof that makes the outcome believable, not as a moat. Product treats it as the core of the product experience.
- **Free MVP.** Offers wants paid pre-commitments at a real price now. The other two focus on proving usage first (tables paid, guests returning).

### The strongest version the board can see

This is **not** the ecosystem as pitched. It's a single product for independent restaurants in one Georgian city: the guest scans a QR code at the table and pays in the browser, with no app needed. That payment automatically creates a verified, dish-level review and a guest record, so the owner sees "Nino, third visit, always orders khinkali" and can switch on a ready-made loyalty reward with one tap. It's sold to owners on one promise: more repeat guests, measured. The payment-linked data is the one asset none of the members thinks a listing site can easily copy. Once that loop dominates one city, the diner directory grows out of the reviews it creates, and the B2B modules (suppliers, hiring, property) become upsells to restaurants that already trust the platform. Those modules come later; they aren't the starting point.

### Chair's note after the founder's clarification (not re-scored)

The founder clarified that the B2B side is a **listings marketplace**: it connects restaurants with suppliers, job seekers and property owners or brokers, and doesn't run the deals itself.

**What this changes**
- Building and running it gets much lighter. Listings plus contact is a far smaller build than procurement, recruitment or brokerage software, so the "ten products" critique is partly answered for the B2B side.
- It could solve the week-one value problem the Offers lens raised. A restaurant gets something useful (staff, suppliers, premises) before any diner has the app. That makes B2B listings a possible free hook to sign up restaurants, not just a later upsell.

**What it doesn't change**
- Every listing board is still its own two-sided market. It needs supply (suppliers, job seekers, property listings) and demand (restaurants), and each side has its own cold start.
- Listings are easy to copy, and both sides can switch away cheaply, because people post in several places at once. Listings alone don't build a moat. The payment-linked guest data is still the only moat the board identified.
- Subscription revenue from listings depends on getting results, such as hires made or supplier deals closed. Willingness to pay is still unknown (→ /founder-consumer, /founder-pricing).

### 5. The gap

**The parts are all available in Tbilisi, but nobody connects them.**

- Payment in the browser with no app install is **already solved and licensed** (Keepz). Building your own payments would be fighting a solved problem. Partner with Keepz, or with a bank, instead.
- Loyalty for independent venues **already exists and is cheap** (Wox, 24–59 GEL). "Loyalty without developing" is not a gap on its own.
- Online booking **is still thin.** Rezto has about 126 partners and Javshani 26, out of roughly 2,562 restaurants on Tripadvisor. Most bookings still go through phone, Instagram or WhatsApp.
- **Nobody ties the payment to the review and the guest profile.** SQR explicitly says *"Can guests pay from their phone? No."* Wox Loyalty is a stamp card a waiter stamps by hand, not linked to what the guest paid for. Tripadvisor and Google reviews are unverified. No one offers dish-level reviews that only come from a real payment.

**So the gap you can own:** one QR code on the table → pay (through Keepz or a bank) → automatic dish-level review + guest record + loyalty points, with no app and no stamping by hand. Sell it as a layer that works alongside the POS a restaurant already has (Wox, SQR, others), not as a replacement for it. **Evidence strength:** the "nobody connects them" part comes from the vendors' own pages and is strong. Restaurant demand for it is not proven yet; that's for `/founder-consumer`.

**Your B2B hook is weak where incumbents are big.** Property listings face ss.ge and myhome.ge, with tens of thousands of listings. Jobs face jobs.ge and hr.ge, which are full of HoReCa vacancies. A HoReCa-only niche can still attract owners (it's filtered and relevant), but don't expect listings to win on volume. A supplier directory has no clear local incumbent in this research and may be the strongest B2B hook. That's a lead to check, not a confirmed finding.

### 6. The threat: who could copy this fastest

1. **Wox.** It already has POS, QR menu and loyalty, and the restaurant relationships. Adding "pay at table via Keepz → review" is a short step for it. This is the main threat.
2. **Keepz.** It owns the payment and already offers tipping. Adding a review prompt and a guest history after payment is a small feature for it.
3. **Bank of Georgia / TBC.** They have the diners (Bank of Georgia reports more than 1.8 million digital monthly users, per [Omno](https://omno.com/payment-methods/keepz/)) and the merchant terminals. If either bank adds receipt-based reviews and loyalty to its app, it reaches every diner on day one.
4. **Rezto.** It has the diner-side booking app and restaurant partners, and could add payment and reviews.

**Defence:** move faster into a single neighbourhood, own the dish-level review data and the diner-facing brand, and integrate with Wox, SQR and Keepz instead of competing with them. Consider a partnership with Keepz from day one.

### Founder's response on B2B listings (17 Nov 2026)

The founder's position: the B2B listings are a free addition. Their value is **focus**: properties suited to restaurants only, jobs for waiters and chefs only, and suppliers, all in the one place where an owner already manages daily operations. They don't need to beat ss.ge or jobs.ge on volume.

What this means for the analysis:
- This is a valid vertical-niche argument. A filtered board can beat a general one on relevance, and it costs the restaurant nothing.
- **It's only useful if it isn't empty.** A niche board with 12 listings is worse than ss.ge with 29,000. Each board needs its supply side seeded before launch: suppliers, job seekers, and property owners or brokers. Don't copy listings from ss.ge, myhome.ge or jobs.ge without permission; that likely breaks their terms.
- **How often an owner uses each board matters for the "daily routine" claim.** Suppliers are used weekly or even daily (orders, prices). Hiring is periodic, and turnover in restaurants is high. Property is rare, used once every few years. Only suppliers fit "daily routine". Jobs and property bring an owner back occasionally.
- Suggested order to fill the boards: (1) suppliers, used most often and with no clear local leader; (2) HoReCa jobs, since restaurants post them themselves, so supply arrives free; (3) property, seeded through a few brokers who specialise in restaurant spaces.

- [ ] **Payments:** you don't hold guest money; the licensed partner does. Keep it that way, or you'd need your own National Bank licence.
- [ ] **Contracts:** the restaurant agreement (subscription, guarantee, private-ratings and data-ownership clauses, termination, exporting data on exit), the board terms (no fake listings, moderation rights), the diner terms and privacy notice.
- [ ] **Advertising and consumer rules** for the guarantee and "free" claims: have a lawyer check them.
