# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **One real candidate for a moat.** Reviews that only come from a paid order, rated dish by dish, plus a visit history per diner, would be data no listing site can copy without also handling the payment. If QR payment becomes the default way to settle the bill, every transaction adds to that data, and each restaurant's loyalty program then depends on the platform. That gives network effects and switching costs, which is rare for a small-country startup.
- **The small-market instinct is right.** Georgia, independent restaurants, one city, is the kind of small market this lens looks for, as long as the founder actually narrows to it.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No small market to own, because it is seven businesses.** Supplier sourcing, hiring, property listings, a directory, bookings, payments and loyalty/CRM each have their own buyers and their own incumbents. This lens wants a big share of a small market. Spreading across seven fronts means a small share of each, every one of them a fight. The pitch does not name a starting city.
2. **A cold start on every side at once, with distribution not addressed.** Diners have no reason to open the app until restaurants are on it and taking QR payments. Restaurants have no reason to pay until diners show up. Suppliers will only pay once restaurants are there. Making the MVP free hides this problem without solving it. Nothing says how the first 100 restaurants or the first 10,000 diners arrive, or what each costs.
3. **No secret and no 10x.** Every feature exists somewhere already as a separate product. The brief names no proprietary technology, no unique access and no insight others lack, and the team is OPEN. If a regional booking or delivery platform, or a bank offering QR payments, adds dish-level reviews in month six, nothing stops it. The one moat (payment-linked data) only forms after scale, and scale is the unsolved part.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Pick ONE module and ONE city. Which is it, and why does that one win alone, with no other modules?
- Who sells this within reach of a Tbilisi (or other) restaurant today, and why are they not already doing it? (/founder-competitors)
- How do the first 100 restaurants sign up, who signs them, and what does each one cost? (/founder-cfo, /founder-competitors)
- Will diners actually pay by QR and leave a review after eating? What share? (/founder-consumer)
- What do QR payments cost to process, and what does it take to get licensed for them in Georgia? Is the founder becoming a payments company? (/founder-cfo)
- How many independent restaurants are in the starting city? Is 100 GEL/month realistic for them? (/founder-competitors, /founder-consumer)
- What does the founder know that local incumbents do not? Who is on the team, and can they build payments-grade software?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable (PASS). What would reopen it:
- Cut to a single wedge, most plausibly QR payment plus verified dish reviews plus loyalty, in one named city. Park sourcing, HR and property until that wedge is dominant.
- Evidence of distribution: a named path to the first 100 restaurants, with a cost per restaurant.
- A competitor map showing the wedge is not already owned locally, and a credible answer to "what stops a copycat in month six".
- A named team that can deliver payments.
