# Product lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- There is one sharp idea inside this pitch: **pay at the table by QR, and that payment creates a verified, dish-level review and tells the restaurant "this guest is back for the third time."** The order is the proof, so the reviews can be trusted, and the restaurant gets guest recognition and loyalty without building any software. That loop is a clean experience on both sides, and the data it creates is the "shared data" the pitch depends on.
- Starting with independent restaurants is the right first customer. They have no CRM, no loyalty system and no in-house tech, so one product that does those things for them is easy to understand.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus. These are about ten products wearing one name.** Supplier sourcing, hiring, property listings, a restaurant directory, booking, payments, reviews, guest profiles, loyalty and campaigns each need their own users, their own content and their own support. Even the one-line pitch needs two clauses and the word "ecosystem," which means the product is not clear yet. Built all at once, every module comes out mediocre, and a mediocre payment flow or booking flow loses the diner for good.
2. **The diner's first five minutes are not designed.** Why would a diner in Georgia download an app to pay a bill? Scanning a QR code that opens an app-store prompt is friction at the moment people least want it: the end of the meal, with friends waiting. If diners don't pay through it, there are no verified reviews, no profiles, no loyalty and nothing to sell to restaurants. The whole B2B value depends on this one moment working.
3. **The seams.** QR payment means handing off between the app, the restaurant's existing POS, the waiter, the bank or acquirer, and the receipt. Splitting a bill, tips, a payment failure mid-scan, a refund, and a review that should belong to whoever ordered the dish are all places where a two-sided system breaks, and each break gets blamed on the platform. The pitch does not say who owns these touchpoints.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, with no "and": what is the one thing this does brilliantly?
- Walk me through a diner's visit, second by second, from sitting down to leaving. When does the app get installed, and why?
- Does QR payment work without installing an app (a web checkout)? Does it connect to the POS systems Georgian restaurants already use? Which ones? (/founder-competitors, /founder-ops)
- What does a restaurant owner see on day one with zero diners in the app? Why do they keep it?
- What will you leave out of version 1 (HR, property, sourcing, campaigns)?
- What does payment processing cost per transaction, and who pays it? (/founder-cfo)
- Do diners actually want to leave reviews per dish, or is that the restaurant's wish? (/founder-consumer)
- Which city, and how many independent restaurants does it have? (/founder-competitors)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Cut to one product:** QR pay-at-table, plus a verified dish-level review, plus repeat-guest recognition for the restaurant. Shelve HR, property, sourcing, campaigns and the full directory until this loop works in one city.
2. **Script the diner's first five minutes** and prove there is no app-install wall at payment (web checkout first, app optional).
3. **Map every handoff** (POS, waiter, acquirer, receipt, refunds, split bills), naming the owner and the failure path for each, before the build starts.
4. **Run a pilot in a handful of restaurants in one named city** and come back with how many tables paid through the product and how many came back a second time.
