# Product lens

Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- The experience fits in one sentence: post one brief, get verified crews to bid, and pay in stages as each step is signed off.
- Focus is disciplined: flat renovation only, one city first, new houses deferred.
- The standard brief is the best decision in the file. It makes bids comparable and turns a messy search into a choice.
- Staged payment after sign-off answers the owner's main fear: paying a stranger for work they cannot check.

## What would kill it (top 3, most dangerous first)

1. Thin supply at the first moment. There are 1 to 4 informal crews and no written agreement. An owner who gets one bid, or none, has no real choice and no reason to return. The number of verified crews in Tbilisi for flat work is unknown, and "verified" is not yet defined.
2. The trust step is undesigned. Money flow is undecided between manual stage payments and platform escrow. The most sensitive moment, paying, sits at a seam between the platform, the crew and a payment partner. If the branch is chosen late, staged payment is a sentence on a slide, not something the owner can see working.
3. The brief does not fit the job. "Flat renovation" spans a bathroom refresh to a full gut. A single template that misses what crews need to price the work makes bids incomparable, and "help choose" becomes guesswork.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- In one sentence, what does the owner feel when they pay the final stage?
- Walk me through the first five minutes: what the owner types, how many fields, in which language, and how long it takes.
- How many bids does an owner need before they can choose? How many verified crews can you name in Tbilisi for flat work today?
- What makes a crew "verified", who checks, and how long does that take per crew?
- In intro-only mode, who holds the money between stages? Which step happens off the platform?
- What happens when a stage is disputed or the scope changes mid-job?
- What do you cut so the pilot is one thing: one city, one job size, one owner type?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. Write the full flow once (brief, bids, choice, contract, each payment, sign-off, close) and name the owner of every handoff. Every seam gets an owner.
2. Choose the money-flow branch before building anything. The owner must be able to see when money moves and who holds it.
3. Test the standard brief with 5 Tbilisi owners and 3 crews before building the platform. Keep only the fields crews need to bid.
4. Get written letters of intent from the crews for the pilot, and agree a minimum bid count before launch. Three is my suggested figure, not a known fact.
5. Put the pilot scope in writing: flat renovation, one city, one owner type. No new houses in the pilot.
