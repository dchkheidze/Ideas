# Monopoly lens

Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The scope is narrow on purpose: one city, flat renovations, one buyer type. Owning a small market first is the right shape for this lens.
- The business does not compete on price. Its edge is trust and process: one standard brief, verified crews, staged money after sign-off. In an informal market, that could become a recognised brand.
- Owners and crews make a two-sided market, which can build a network effect. That only works once both sides are dense in one district, and the file shows no such density yet.
- If the platform holds the money, it stays in the middle of every job. That is the strongest structural point in the file, and it is still undecided.

## What would kill it (top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Disintermediation.** Once an owner and a crew meet through the platform, nothing in the plan stops them from signing the next job directly. The builder fee is charged per won contract, so every repeat job is a fee the platform can lose. Only platform-held money, or a real reason the owner needs the platform after sign-off, keeps the middle in place. The file leaves that choice open.
2. **No known way to reach the first 100 owners.** The file names acquisition channels as unknown and gives no cost per owner. Flat renovations are probably infrequent for any one owner, so each customer may be a one-off that has to be won again. Without a cost per owner, nobody can tell whether one fee per job pays for finding it.
3. **Thin supply presented as a marketplace.** With 1 to 4 informal crews, "verified crews bid" may mean one or two crews in practice. A single-crew referral service is not a marketplace. Any builder with a copy of the standard brief and contract could match it, and the file says nothing about what stops that in month six: no data, no exclusive crew terms, no brand yet.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which city, and how many owners in it renovate in a typical year? Where does that number come from?
- How does the first owner arrive, and what does that owner cost in money and time? What about the 100th?
- Who already serves Tbilisi owners who renovate (crews' own networks, agencies, online groups)? Name three and say what each does better.
- What stops a crew from taking the owner's number after one job? What makes the platform worth its fee on the second job?
- What do the 1 to 4 crews agree to, even informally? Any lead routing, minimum volume or exclusivity?
- The file says trade knowledge and crew access come from partners. What does the founder know about owners or crews that others do not?
- If escrow is chosen, who keeps the record of completed jobs and disputes? Could that record be the reason both sides stay?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. The money flow is decided before the pilot: platform-held escrow, or a written reason why introduction-only still keeps owner and crew on the platform after sign-off.
2. A measured cost per owner from a small paid test, and a fee model that covers it at the planned volume, checked by the CFO.
3. Written agreements, even if simple, with the crews covering lead routing, a minimum job count and no fee bypass on direct deals.
4. One named district of Tbilisi, with counted owners and crews in it, before any expansion.
5. The founder's own edge in one sentence, and a first copy-resistant asset (the completion and dispute record, or exclusive crew terms) with a date to build it.
