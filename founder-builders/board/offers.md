# Offers lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The pain is real and costly. Building a house or renovating a flat is a rare, large and risky purchase, and private buyers often cannot judge a bid. An offer can sell into that.
- The outcome is easy to state: a finished job, on a written contract, with the money handled. A buyer can grasp that in one sentence.
- The spend per job is large by nature. The actual size, and whether it differs by city, is not known yet.
- The founder names two existing platforms in the same space (pzzl.ru, stroim.dom.rf). Their traction is unknown to this board and should be checked before anyone leans on them.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No proof the result will be good.** The promise is a finished job, but the idea does not say how builders are verified, what past work buyers see, or whether money waits until the work is done. Without that, the buyer's belief in the outcome is weak and they fall back to word of mouth.
2. **The commission gets bypassed.** The platform earns on the contract, but after the first introduction the buyer and builder can sign off-platform. If the introduction is the only thing the platform adds, the buyer has a reason to skip the fee.
3. **Two offers in one.** New houses and flat renovation differ in ticket size, timeline and buyer. Launching both spreads builder supply and acquisition spend thin, and makes the outcome harder to promise.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which city, and which one segment first: new house or flat renovation?
- What does a typical job cost the customer, and how long does it take today from the first request to a signed contract?
- Who pays the commission, builder or customer, and at what level? What is the lowest level builders would accept?
- Is money held in escrow, or do you only introduce the parties? What happens if a builder stops mid-job?
- What guarantee does the platform stand behind, and who pays when it is used?
- How many builders in that city can you sign before the first customer, and what do they pay for leads now?
- What would make a customer pay you rather than calling the builder directly?
- Could you double the fee and still be the obvious choice? What would have to be true?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Choose one city and one segment. Prove the offer there before any second market.
2. Write the value equation with numbers: the target time from request to signed contract, and the effort the customer must spend. Both must beat what the buyer does today.
3. Decide escrow or introduction only. State the guarantee in one sentence a customer can repeat, and confirm with the CFO that it is affordable.
4. Verify builders before listing them, and show their past work to customers.
5. Sign builders and collect waiting customers in the chosen city before building the full product. Agree the targets and a cost-per-customer ceiling with the founder; treat none of them as known until measured.
6. Confirm the commission level with builders in a test before locking it in.
