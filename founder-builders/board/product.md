# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The problem is specific. A private customer who wants a house built or a flat renovated has no simple way to compare several builders and to be protected in the deal. The idea names that moment.
- The flow has a natural shape: describe the job, receive bids, choose one, sign, pay. A platform that owns every step could be the one place the customer goes.
- Flat renovation is in scope from day one. It is probably smaller and shorter than a new build, which could give a faster first result to learn from.

## What would kill it (top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Too many products at once.** The idea covers new houses, flat renovation, bidding, contracts and payments. Each has a different buyer, timeline and trust problem. Building all of them means none works well. Focus means choosing one job and saying no to the rest until it works.
2. **The money seam is undecided.** If the platform only introduces the parties, it has no reason to stay in the loop after the handoff, and the customer signs elsewhere. If it holds the money, the product must carry milestones, disputes and refunds, a much larger build. The handoff from bid to contract to payment is where the experience will break first.
3. **Bids the customer cannot compare.** The customer's key decision is choosing a builder from bids. If each builder scopes the job differently, the bids are just prices, and the customer falls back to price or a referral. The idea does not say how a job is described so that bids match.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is the one job the first customer finishes on the platform?
- Walk me through the first five minutes from the customer's side. What do they type, and what do they see within 24 hours?
- Does the platform hold the money? What happens when the customer and the builder disagree about a finished stage?
- If you could launch with only one of new build or flat renovation, which one, and why?
- Who writes the job description, and how are bids compared on the same scope?
- What happens after the contract is signed? Does the customer stay on the platform until completion?
- Which city and language? Local contract and payment norms will shape the flow, so this is needed before design.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Name one job type and one city for launch, and write the one-sentence promise.
2. Decide escrow or introduction before any design, and draw the money flow on one page.
3. Map the full journey as steps. Mark each handoff (bid to contract, contract to payment, change orders, completion, disputes) with an owner.
4. Create a standard job brief and bid template so that bids compare on the same scope.
5. Test the flow by hand on a few real jobs (a form, a spreadsheet, phone calls) before building the app.
